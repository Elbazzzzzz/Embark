How to Prepare Your Dog for Group Walks

By Joe Hewison · 22 January 2026

Group walks are one of the best things you can offer a sociable dog, exercise, enrichment and calm companionship with a small, steady pack, all in the kind of woodland that Tunbridge Wells does so well. But they're not right for every dog straight away, and that's absolutely fine. The question we hear most often at consultations is simple: is my dog ready?

At Embark, we keep our requirements clear and consistent. They're not there to be awkward. They're there to keep every dog in the group safe, calm and happy. This guide walks through the three things that matter most: socialisation, off-lead recall, and neutering. If your dog is working towards any of these, you'll find practical advice here. If they're already there, you're probably ready for a free consultation.

Why preparation matters

Our group walks take place in woodland and forest locations around Royal Tunbridge Wells. Dogs are off lead by design, that's a core part of what we offer. A pavement walk on a short lead is a very different experience from an hour in Hargate Forest with a carefully matched group of steady, friendly dogs. The physical space is bigger, the distractions are different, and your dog needs the skills to navigate that environment safely.

Each group is carefully selected and limited to ensure the best possible walk, and the safety of all dogs. That means we can supervise properly, match temperaments thoughtfully, and make sure nobody gets overwhelmed. Even so, the group dynamic only works if every member is comfortable around other dogs and responsive when called. Preparation isn't about perfection. It's about honest assessment and building the right foundations.

Socialisation: comfortable around other dogs

The first requirement is straightforward: all dogs on Embark group walks must be well socialised and comfortable around other dogs. That doesn't mean your dog has to love every dog they meet or play endlessly with the group. It does mean they can share space calmly, passing another dog on a path, waiting while the pack gathers, settling when another dog trots past, without becoming anxious, reactive or over-excited to the point of losing focus.

Good socialisation is built gradually. If your dog is young or hasn't spent much time around other dogs, start small. Arrange controlled introductions with one calm, friendly dog in a neutral space. Keep sessions short and positive. Watch for relaxed body language: loose wagging, soft eyes, a willingness to sniff and move on. Stiff posture, excessive barking, lunging or hiding behind your legs are signs you need more groundwork before a group walk is the right step.

Some dogs are naturally sociable; others need more time. Neither is a problem. It's about knowing where your dog sits on that spectrum. At our free consultation, we'll meet your dog and have an honest conversation about whether they're ready for a woodland group, or whether a bit more one-to-one work would help first.

Off-lead recall: the non-negotiable

Of our three requirements, recall is the one that catches people out most often. All dogs on Embark group walks must be able to walk off lead with reliable recall. Our woodland walks are off lead by design. It's central to the enrichment we offer. A dog who can't be called back reliably in open woodland is not safe in a group setting, however friendly they are.

Reliable recall doesn't mean your dog comes back perfectly every single time in every environment. It does mean that in the kinds of places we walk, woodland tracks, open heath, forest clearings, your dog returns promptly when called, even with moderate distractions. Squirrels, other walkers, interesting scents: your dog should be able to break away and come back to you without a long chase or repeated calling.

Building recall takes practice. Start in a secure area with low distractions and reward generously every time your dog returns. Gradually increase difficulty: longer distances, mild distractions, then more challenging environments. Use a consistent cue, one word, one tone, and make coming back to you the best thing that happens on the walk. If recall is patchy, work on it before applying for group walks. It's kinder to your dog and fairer to the rest of the pack.

Not sure how your dog's recall would hold up off lead in woodland? That's exactly what the consultation is for. We can discuss where your dog is now and whether group walks are the right fit, or whether a few weeks of focused recall work would set them up for success.

Neutering: keeping the group calm

Our third requirement: dogs over one year old must be neutered to join group walks. This helps keep the group calm and safe for everyone. It's a policy we've held consistently because mixed groups of entire dogs, particularly males, can create tension that has nothing to do with poor training or bad temperament. Hormones affect behaviour, and in a small supervised group, we need predictable, steady dynamics.

If your dog is under one year old, talk to us at the consultation. Puppies and young adolescents have different needs, and we're happy to discuss timing and whether one-to-one walking makes more sense while you wait. For dogs over one year, neutering is a firm requirement before joining the regular pack.

We know neutering is a personal decision and owners sometimes have questions about timing, breed-specific considerations or their vet's advice. We're not here to lecture. We're here to be clear about what works for our group walks. If you're on the fence, your vet is the right person for the medical conversation; we're the right people for the walking conversation.

Putting it all together

A dog who's ready for Embark group walks is well socialised, recalls reliably off lead, and (if over one year) is neutered. They don't need to be flawless. They need to be safe, steady and comfortable in the environment we walk in. That's the bar, and it's the same for every dog in the group.

If you're ticking all three boxes, the next step is simple. Contact me to arrange a free consultation. We'll come meet you and your dog, talk through your routine and answer any questions about pick-up times, pricing, locations and what a typical walk looks like. There's no obligation, just an honest chat about whether we're the right fit for each other.
