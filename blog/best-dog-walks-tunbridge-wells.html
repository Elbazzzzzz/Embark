The 5 Best Dog Walks in Tunbridge Wells

By Joe Hewison · 15 January 2026

If you live in Royal Tunbridge Wells, you already know the town has more to offer dogs than a quick pavement loop. I'm lucky to sit on the edge of the High Weald, an Area of Outstanding Natural Beauty, with ancient woodland and rolling countryside within minutes of TN1. After eight years of walking dogs here professionally, these are the five woodland spots I keep coming back to.

Whether you're planning a weekend adventure with your own dog or wondering where a professional walker heads with the pack, this guide covers the routes that deliver real enrichment: varied terrain, interesting scents, space to run, and enough quiet corners to keep even sensitive dogs happy.

1. Hargate Forest

Hargate Forest is one of the finest woodland walks in the High Weald, and it's a regular on my Embark rotation. Mature trees, wide forestry tracks and plenty of undergrowth give dogs a proper workout for body and nose. The paths are generally well defined, which makes it easier to keep sight of your dog while still giving them freedom to explore off lead, provided, of course, that their recall is reliable.

What makes Hargate special is the sense of space. Unlike tighter urban parks, dogs can spread out, sniff at their own pace and enjoy the kind of calm woodland atmosphere that tires them out far more effectively than mileage alone. After rain, the forest floor comes alive with interesting scents, brilliant for mental stimulation. In dry spells, the shade of the canopy keeps walks comfortable even on warmer days.

2. Cinderhill

Cinderhill is quiet woodland with the kind of scent-rich tracks dogs remember. It is less of a destination park and more of a proper forest walk: leaf litter underfoot, natural obstacles and enough room for the pack to explore off lead without feeling hemmed in.

I like Cinderhill because it rewards curiosity. Dogs get to work their noses, pick their own pace and settle into a steady woodland rhythm. It is a strong choice when the group needs enrichment as much as exercise, and it sits comfortably within the wider High Weald landscape I rotate through each week.

3. Pembury Woods

Pembury Woods sits east of Royal Tunbridge Wells and is a natural fit when I am collecting dogs from Pembury and the surrounding roads. Shaded rides, woodland loops and open stretches between the trees make it well suited to a full hour of off-lead walking.

For dogs who know town pavements better than forest trails, Pembury Woods is a step up: cooler under the canopy in summer, full of new smells after wet weather, and calm enough for carefully selected groups to enjoy without the bustle of a formal park. It is one of the routes that makes the east side of my service area such a pleasure to walk.

4. Snipe Woods

Snipe Woods offers mixed woodland and varied ground, exactly the sort of terrain that tires a dog out in the right way. Roots, rises, soft forest floor and changing scents keep brains busy while legs get a genuine workout.

I rotate Snipe Woods into the schedule when conditions and the group suit a deeper woodland session. Sensitive dogs often settle well here: there is space to spread out, interesting ground to explore, and less of the constant foot traffic you find closer to the centre of town.

5. Brenchley Woods

Brenchley Woods is classic High Weald forest: mature trees, quieter paths and the sense that you have left the town behind. It is a favourite when I want a calm, enrichment-led walk with room for the pack to move naturally off lead.

Like the rest of my rotation, I do not hammer the same trail every day. Brenchley Woods gives me another strong woodland option so dogs get novelty, soft ground and real countryside air. Weather and ground conditions decide when I head there, but it earns its place among the five walks I return to most often.

What makes a great dog walk?

A good walk isn't just about distance. Dogs need mental stimulation, socialisation and the chance to express natural behaviours, sniffing, exploring, trotting at their own pace. Woodland beats pavement on every one of those measures. Off-lead time, where it's safe and your dog's recall allows it, transforms a walk from exercise into enrichment.

That's the philosophy behind my group dog walking service. I skip the quick block laps and head straight for the good stuff, the same woodlands and forests covered in this guide. Each group is carefully selected and limited to ensure the best possible walk and the safety of all dogs, with a minimum of one hour, pick-up and drop-off included. Every dog on my walks needs reliable off-lead recall and solid social skills, because these environments are off lead by design.
