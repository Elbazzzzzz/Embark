Where I Walk

I skip the pavement laps and head straight for the good stuff: woodlands, forests and open countryside within easy reach of Royal Tunbridge Wells.

Woodland Over Pavement

A dog walking around the block gets exercise. A dog walking through woodland gets a workout for their body, brain and nose. Varied terrain, new scents, natural obstacles and space to run off lead. That's what makes an Embark walk different.

All dogs need more than just physical exercise. They also have emotional, mental and behavioural needs. Woodland walks tick every box: socialisation with a small, steady group, mental stimulation from sniffing and exploring, and the calm that comes from being out in nature rather than on a busy pavement.

My Walking Locations

Hargate Forest: One of my favourite spots in the High Weald. Mature woodland, wide tracks and plenty of room for the pack to spread out and explore safely off lead.

Cinderhill: Quiet woodland tracks with room to roam. A regular on my rotation for dogs who thrive on scent-rich forest floors and steady off-lead time.

Pembury Woods: Shaded woodland east of town, with rides and loops that suit a full hour of exploration. Ideal when I am collecting from the Pembury side of my patch.

Snipe Woods: Mixed woodland with natural undergrowth and varied terrain. Brilliant for mental stimulation as well as a proper physical workout.

Brenchley Woods: Classic High Weald forest, mature trees, quiet paths and space for the pack to settle into a calm, enrichment-led walk.

I rotate locations to keep walks interesting and to avoid overusing any single spot. Weather, ground conditions and the group on the day all play a part in where I head. Read more about local routes in Helpful Info: The 5 Best Dog Walks in Tunbridge Wells.
